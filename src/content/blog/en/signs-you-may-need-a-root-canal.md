What are the signs you need a root canal? Usually, they point to the pulp inside your tooth, the soft tissue containing nerves and blood vessels, being inflamed or infected. The most common warning signs are a toothache that lingers or starts for no obvious reason, sensitivity to hot or cold that does not fade quickly, pain when you bite, a tooth that darkens, or swelling on the gum. These symptoms can also have other causes, though, and only an examination with an X-ray can show what is really going on.

Whether you live in Benidorm or are here on holiday, it is worth knowing what to look out for.

## What is a root canal treatment, and why is it done?

A root canal treatment, also called endodontic treatment, involves cleaning and sealing the inside of a tooth whose pulp has been damaged. Each tooth has one or more narrow canals running down to the root. If bacteria reach the pulp, it can become inflamed or infected.

Common causes include:

- deep decay that reaches the pulp;
- a crack or fracture in the tooth;
- a knock or injury to the tooth;
- repeated dental work on the same tooth.

The aim of a root canal is to try to save your natural tooth rather than remove it. You can read more about how we approach this on our [root canal treatment in Benidorm](/en/root-canal-treatment/) page.

## Signs you may need a root canal

No single symptom on its own confirms that you need a root canal. Still, these are the signs that are worth getting checked:

- **A persistent or spontaneous toothache.** Pain that does not ease, that appears without you eating or drinking anything, or that wakes you at night.
- **Lingering sensitivity to hot or cold.** A brief twinge from an ice-cold drink is common. If the discomfort carries on after the hot or cold has gone, have it looked at.
- **Pain when biting or pressing.** A tooth that is tender when you chew or touch it may mean the tissues around the root are affected.
- **A darkened tooth.** A tooth turning grey or darker than its neighbours can be a sign of damage inside, sometimes after an old knock.
- **Swelling or a small bump on the gum.** Puffiness near the root, or a pimple-like bump that comes and goes, can be linked to infection.
- **Tenderness in the gum.** Soreness when you touch the area around one particular tooth.

## Pain is not always the only sign

It is easy to assume that if the pain has stopped, the problem has gone. Sometimes the pulp is so badly damaged that it no longer causes symptoms, so the discomfort fades even though the infection may still be there. Some teeth that need treatment are only discovered at a routine check-up, when the patient has noticed nothing at all.

This is why regular check-ups matter. At our clinic we use [digital dentistry](/en/digital-dentistry/), including digital X-rays and a 3D intraoral scanner, which helps us see the real condition of the tooth and the tissues around it.

## These symptoms can have other causes

Sensitivity, pain when chewing or discomfort in one area does not always mean the pulp is affected. It could be down to a shallow cavity, an irritated gum, a filling that needs checking, teeth grinding or clenching, or a problem with a nearby tooth.

That is why self-diagnosis is not a good idea. Your dentist will assess your symptoms, examine the tooth and, usually, take an X-ray to decide whether a root canal is needed or whether another treatment will do.

## What does a root canal involve?

Every case is different and your dentist will explain your own plan, but in general the procedure goes like this:

1. Local anaesthetic is given so the area is numb during the treatment.
2. The dentist opens the tooth and removes the damaged or infected pulp.
3. The canals are cleaned and disinfected.
4. They are filled and sealed to stop bacteria getting back in.
5. The tooth is restored. A filling or, often, a crown is placed, especially on back teeth, to protect it when you chew.

Depending on the case, treatment may take more than one visit. Some tenderness in the area for a few days afterwards is normal, and your dentist will give you the advice you need. When a tooth needs a crown or a larger rebuild, this is where [oral rehabilitation](/en/oral-rehabilitation/) comes in.

## A common myth: "a root canal is very painful"

This idea is still widespread, but with modern local anaesthesia the procedure is generally comfortable for most patients. In fact, the pain people remember usually comes from the infection or inflammation beforehand, not from the treatment itself. If you feel nervous, tell your dentist before you start: they can explain what you will feel and answer your questions.

## Why you should not wait

A damaged pulp does not usually heal by itself. Left alone, the infection can spread to the tissues around the root, which may complicate treatment or make it harder to save the tooth. Getting checked when the first warning signs appear generally gives you more options.

If, along with the toothache, you have a fever, swelling in the face that is spreading, difficulty swallowing or breathing, or pain that is getting worse quickly, do not wait for a routine appointment: contact the clinic or the emergency services (112) straight away.
