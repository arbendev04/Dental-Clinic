Braces or clear aligners: which should you choose? There is no single answer. Both systems move teeth in a controlled way, but they work differently and do not suit every case equally well. Fixed braces give very precise control, even in complex cases. Clear aligners are removable, transparent trays that are far more discreet, but they only work if you wear them consistently. The right choice depends on how your teeth sit, how your bite works and how you live day to day, and only an assessment with your dentist can tell you which option makes sense for you.

## What they have in common

Before comparing them, it helps to know what they share. Braces and aligners are both forms of [orthodontic treatment](/en/orthodontics/): they apply gentle, steady pressure to gradually guide teeth into a better position. The aim is not only cosmetic. Well-aligned teeth are usually easier to keep clean and help your bite spread chewing forces more evenly.

Both systems need a proper assessment beforehand, regular check-ups during treatment and, once it ends, a retention phase so the teeth do not drift back.

## How braces work

Braces are small brackets bonded to the surface of the teeth and linked by a wire. At each check-up, your dentist adjusts the wire to keep guiding the movement.

Worth knowing:

- **They are fixed.** You cannot take them out, so treatment keeps progressing even when you forget about them.
- **They offer a lot of control.** They can move teeth in several directions at once, which is useful for significant crowding or more complex bite problems.
- **There are more discreet versions.** As well as metal braces, there are tooth-coloured brackets that are less noticeable.
- **They need a bit more care when eating and cleaning.** Very hard or sticky foods can loosen a bracket, and cleaning takes longer and may need special brushes.

## How clear aligners work

Aligners are custom-made trays of clear plastic. Each set moves the teeth a little, and you switch to the next one following the plan your dentist designs. They are made from a very precise record of your mouth: at our clinic we use [digital dentistry](/en/digital-dentistry/) with a 3D intraoral scanner, so there is no need for traditional impression putty.

Worth knowing:

- **They are almost invisible.** This is why many adults prefer them.
- **They are removable.** You take them out to eat, to drink anything other than water and to brush your teeth, which makes cleaning easier.
- **They depend on you.** They only work while you are wearing them. Your dentist will tell you how many hours a day to wear them, and skipping that time delays or undermines the result.
- **They are not right for every case.** Some movements are achieved more reliably with braces. Your dentist will assess whether your case is suitable for aligners.

## Braces vs aligners: the key differences

In a nutshell:

- **Appearance:** aligners are harder to spot; tooth-coloured braces sit somewhere in between.
- **Everyday comfort:** aligners have no parts that rub, although you may feel some pressure for a few days when you change trays. With braces, a little irritation is common at first.
- **Food:** with aligners you can eat anything, because you take them out. With braces, some foods are best avoided.
- **Cleaning:** simpler with aligners; braces need more attention.
- **Discipline:** braces work on their own; aligners need your commitment.
- **Type of case:** braces usually have more scope in complex cases.

Neither option wins on every count. Each has its strengths depending on the case and the person.

## What affects the choice

When we assess an orthodontic case, we usually consider:

1. **The type of problem.** Slight crowding in the front teeth is not the same as a crossbite or a problem between the jaws.
2. **Age and development.** In children and teenagers, growth is also taken into account when planning.
3. **Your routine.** If you think you will struggle to wear aligners for the time recommended, braces may be more practical.
4. **How much discretion matters to you.** For people who deal with the public at work, this can weigh heavily.
5. **The health of your gums.** Gums need to be healthy before teeth are moved. If there is inflammation, that is treated first.

That is why we do not recommend deciding based only on what you read online or what worked for a friend.

## How long does treatment take?

It depends on each case. The time varies with what needs correcting, how each person responds and, with aligners, whether they are worn as instructed. Your dentist will give you an estimate after studying your mouth and will review it as treatment goes on.

What both systems share is the final stage: once treatment ends, retainers are used to hold the teeth in their new position. Skipping this stage is one of the most common reasons teeth shift again over time.

## A common myth: "orthodontics is only for children"

More and more adults have orthodontic treatment, and there is no upper age limit as long as the teeth and gums are healthy. Many adults want to correct crowding that has worsened over the years, make cleaning easier or prepare their mouth for other treatments, such as [cosmetic dentistry](/en/cosmetic-dentistry/) or replacing a missing tooth. Clear aligners are popular with adults because they are so discreet, although they are not always the right fit.

## Every case is different

Two people whose teeth look similar may need quite different plans. What you see on the surface does not always reflect what is happening in the bite, the roots or the bone. And sometimes what looks like a positioning problem has other causes, such as clenching your teeth, a missing tooth or a gum that has receded.

That is why the first step is always a thorough diagnosis, with an examination and whatever tests your dentist considers necessary. From there, they will explain which options suit your case and what you can expect from each.

## When not to wait

Orthodontics is rarely an emergency, but some situations are worth checking promptly: pain when chewing, loose teeth, gums that bleed often or a tooth that has moved position quickly. If you already have braces or aligners, contact the clinic if a bracket comes loose, a wire is digging in, or an aligner cracks or stops fitting.

If, after a knock to the mouth, you have severe pain, bleeding that will not stop or facial swelling that is getting worse, do not wait: contact the clinic or the emergency services (112) straight away.
