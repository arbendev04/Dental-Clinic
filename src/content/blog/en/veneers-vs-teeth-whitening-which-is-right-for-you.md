The main difference between veneers and teeth whitening is this: whitening lightens the colour of your natural teeth without changing their shape, while veneers alter the look of the visible surface of the tooth, including its colour, shape and, in some cases, its apparent alignment. They are not really rivals, because they solve different problems. Which one suits you depends on exactly what you want to change and on the health of your mouth, which only an examination can tell you.

## What professional teeth whitening is

Whitening lightens the shade of natural teeth using products that act on stains and on the colour of the tooth itself. When it is done professionally, a dentist supervises the process, adapts it to you and first checks that your mouth is ready for it.

It helps to understand its limits:

- **It changes colour, not shape.** If a tooth is small, chipped or twisted, whitening will not correct that.
- **It only works on natural tooth structure.** Crowns, fillings and veneers do not whiten, so they can end up a different shade from the rest of your teeth.
- **Results depend on the type of stain** and on the individual. Not every tooth responds in the same way.
- **It can cause temporary sensitivity** in some patients. If that happens, let your dentist know.

## What dental veneers are

Veneers are very thin shells, usually made of porcelain or composite resin, bonded to the front surface of a tooth. They can change the colour, shape, size and overall appearance of several teeth at once.

Unlike whitening, veneers involve a procedure on the tooth. In many cases a thin layer of enamel has to be removed so the veneer fits properly, which is why it is a decision to weigh up carefully and with full information. Veneers also do not last forever: over time they may need repair or replacement, and they depend on good hygiene and regular check-ups. Ceramic materials generally resist staining well, but they are not immune to wear.

## A quick comparison

**Teeth whitening:** lightens the colour of natural teeth. It does not change shape, size or position, it does not affect crowns, fillings or veneers, and its effect can fade over time depending on your habits.

**Dental veneers:** change the colour, shape and look of the visible surface of the tooth. They involve a procedure on the tooth, usually with a thin layer of enamel removed, and need maintenance and, in time, possible adjustments or replacement.

## Which suits which situation?

These pointers are general. The final decision is always made after an examination:

- **Your teeth are healthy and well shaped, but darker or more yellow than you would like.** Whitening is often the first option to consider, because it is less invasive.
- **You are bothered by the shape or size of a tooth**, or you have small chips, wear or gaps you would like to close. Veneers may be an option.
- **You have stains that do not respond to whitening**, depending on their cause, or teeth in very different shades. Veneers can sometimes give a more even result.
- **Your teeth are slightly out of line.** Veneers can disguise minor irregularities, but in other cases [orthodontics](/en/orthodontics/) may be the better route to move teeth into position. Your dentist will advise what fits your situation.

## Can you combine them?

Yes, and it is quite common to plan them together. Because veneers do not change colour with whitening, the sensible order is to lighten your natural teeth first and, once you are happy with the shade, choose the colour of the veneers to match it. That way the rest of your teeth do not end up darker than the restorations.

If you are considering both, tell your dentist from the start so the steps can be put in the right order and the result looks harmonious. At our clinic, smile design is part of our [cosmetic dentistry](/en/cosmetic-dentistry/), with the aim of a result that looks natural on your face.

## Maintenance and everyday habits

No cosmetic treatment lasts the same length of time for everyone. Some habits clearly make a difference:

- **Coffee, tea, red wine and other strongly coloured drinks** can gradually stain natural teeth and reduce the effect of whitening.
- **Smoking** stains teeth and is bad for gum health.
- **Good daily hygiene and regular check-ups** help keep both natural teeth and veneers in good condition.
- **Avoiding biting hard objects** such as pens, nails or ice protects both enamel and restorations.

Your dentist will give you advice specific to your case. If you notice discomfort, or a veneer feels loose or chipped, do not wait: contact the clinic.

## Why the assessment comes first

Before any cosmetic treatment, your dentist needs to make sure your mouth is healthy. That usually means checking for cavities, making sure your gums are in good condition and looking for underlying problems that should be dealt with first. If your gums are inflamed, for example, they may need [gum treatment](/en/gum-treatment/) first, because cosmetic results depend on a healthy foundation.

The examination is also a chance to talk about what you hope for and what is realistic in your case. The cost varies according to what you need and is explained once the assessment has been carried out.
